# OT6 v0.14 — The Floating Continent

OT6 is a ROM hack of Final Fantasy VI (released in North America as
Final Fantasy III). It is distributed as a BPS patch, a file that
records the differences between the original ROM and the modified ROM.

## How to play

You need a Final Fantasy III (USA) 1.0 ROM (sha1
4f37e4274ac3b2ea1bedb08aa149d8fc5bb676e7). Anything else will be rejected.

The easy way: unzip `ot6-v0.14.zip` and put `Final Fantasy III (USA).bps` in
the same folder as your ROM, keeping both names exactly as they are, then
open the ROM. Most emulators — Mesen, bsnes, Snes9x and others — notice a
patch sitting beside a ROM of the same name and apply it as they load. Your
ROM file is not modified.

If your emulator does not do that, apply the patch yourself with any BPS
patcher (Flips, beat) and open the result instead.

Play from the beginning through the end of the World of Balance — everything
the Thamasa release (v0.13) covered, then the world tour aboard the repaired
Blackjack, the Imperial Air Force gauntlet, the Floating Continent, and the
escape — and stop where the game sets you down in the World of Ruin: Celes
alone in a house on a solitary island. Past that point the new systems may
run but are not part of this release's supported, balanced playtest range.

## What's changed

**The playable range reaches the end of the World of Balance.** This is the
last release of the end-of-WoB arc (v0.12–v0.14): from the massacre's
aftermath you fly the Blackjack, prepare however you like, and take on the
Floating Continent — the IAF gauntlet in the sky, the crossing, Shadow's
rejoining, AtmaWeapon, and the six-minute escape — through the fall of the
World of Balance to the World of Ruin's opening scene. The break system's
authored shields and weaknesses run through every fight in the arc.

**The world is worth a tour before you commit.** The pre-FC World of
Balance is an open checklist, and the route expects you to use some of it:
the Veldt and the open plains support a short, honest grind; Jidoor's
auction house sells Golem and Zoneseek (the bidding is real — walk away from
anything else); Sraphim is cheap in Tzen; and Narshe's cliffs hold Mog, with
the Water Rondo dance learnable on the way. The three optional espers got
the same deliberate stat-teaching pass every other esper has.

**The IAF gauntlet is an attrition fight you can win.** The sky waves carry
authored break rows (Sky Armor and Spit Fire break to slash and pierce),
the game gives you a real breather between waves — use it to heal — and
Ultros picks his moment. Bolt is the arc's keystone element: bring it
taught, bring it boosted.

**The Floating Continent plays like enemy ground.** The chute network, the
one-way ledges, and the random pool all got the authoring pass — seven
species with break rows tuned so a prepared bolt-leaning party breaks
everything it meets. Shadow can be re-hired mid-crossing, and whether he
survives the continent is, as always, up to you.

**AtmaWeapon is the World of Balance's final exam.** Eleven shield pips
behind slash and pierce, over twenty thousand HP, and he re-raises his
guard mid-fight. He is beatable at route level with the kit the continent
hands you — and he will make you spend it. Expect casualties; win anyway.

**The escape is six real minutes, and Shadow is worth five of them.** The
clock runs everywhere — menus and battles included — so move. Nerapa bars
the last stretch (he absorbs fire and hates ice; check your rods before
you swing). And at the ledge, the choice the game has always offered is
still the whole point: you can jump now, or you can wait for Shadow until
five seconds remain. The route's canon is to wait.

## A warning about Sketch

Relm's Sketch still carries Final Fantasy VI 1.0's most famous bug,
deliberately left in place (see the v0.13 notes for the full house-rule
rationale). When a Sketch misses, the game can rarely corrupt your
inventory or save. **Save before experimenting with Sketch.** The world
map saves anywhere.

## What we'd like to know

- The IAF gauntlet: does attrition-plus-breathers feel like a fight or a
  slog? Did the between-wave heals read as intended play?
- AtmaWeapon: he's tuned as a final exam — two knockouts in our own
  clears. Fair peak, or over the line?
- The escape: does six minutes leave room to breathe, fight Nerapa, and
  still wait for Shadow — and did you find the wait on your own?
- Shadow across the whole arc: hiring him, losing him, waiting for him —
  does his thread land?
- Anything odd in the big scenes (the statues, the fall, the landing).
